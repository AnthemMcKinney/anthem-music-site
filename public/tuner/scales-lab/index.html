QUICK DEFINITIONS
Note
One musical sound.
Natural note
One of the seven lettered notes: A, B, C, D, E, F, or G.
Chromatic scale
All twelve notes in order, including the natural notes, sharps, and flats.
Sharp
Raises a note by one half step.
Flat
Lowers a note by one half step.
Pattern
A fixed fretboard shape. One pattern can contain more than one scale, depending on which root feels like home.
CAGED
A map based on the open C, A, G, E, and D chord shapes that divides the neck into five connected boxes.
Root
The note that feels like home and gives a scale its name.
Octave
The same note name at a higher or lower pitch after twelve half steps.
Scale
An ordered collection of notes organized around a root.
Pentatonic scale
A five-note scale that gives us the strongest foundation notes for riffs and solos.
Diatonic scale
A seven-note scale. Here it adds B and F to the pentatonic foundation.
Major scale
A diatonic scale with a major sound. In this pattern, C major resolves on C.
Minor scale
A related diatonic scale with a minor sound. In this pattern, A minor resolves on A.
Tab (tablature)
Six-line guitar notation that shows which string and fret to play.
Beat / rhythm
The steady pulse and timing that turn a group of notes into music.

Natural notes use the seven letter names. After G, the note names begin again at A in a higher octave. Sharps and flats name the pitches between most natural notes. B–C and E–F sit directly next to each other with no note between them.
