A little help tuning

Auto strings chooses the nearest target in your selected tuning. Tap any string to lock its target; tap Auto strings to resume automatic selection. The large note is the pitch actually heard, including its octave. “Too low” means its pitch needs to rise; “Too high” means it needs to fall. The direction you turn the peg depends on the instrument.

If the note is far away, double-check the string and listen to the reference tone before adjusting. Use Lock string to keep a target fixed when the string is far out of tune. Auto mode can only infer the nearest target, not know which physical string you intended. Don’t keep tightening a string if it feels unusually tight.

Use a quiet room and place your device nearby. The tuner follows changing pitches and quiet sustained notes while rejecting unclear sound. The orange line shows the last 3.5 seconds of measured pitch. When detection stops, the pointer holds its horizontal position and the trace holds its last shape in muted gray. The note and trace follow the same confirmed pitch. Brief gaps are bridged; longer pauses are labeled as the last reading. Gaps mean the pitch was unclear. The input bar shows relative microphone signal level on a logarithmic scale, not tuning accuracy or pitch confidence. The trace softens but remains visible during pauses. The note and numeric readings stay visible, labeled LAST READING until fresh pitch is detected; they are not fresh measurements. Reference playback pauses listening briefly. The pointer begins blending toward green within 10 cents. Full green and the ding require an average within ±5 cents across three consistent readings, with brief microphone gaps allowed. A4 = 440 Hz. The trace shows smoothed pitch variation, not the audio waveform.

On a phone, the microphone requires a trusted HTTPS connection. Once installed and cached, the tuner can work offline. If access is blocked, allow the microphone in your browser’s site settings.
