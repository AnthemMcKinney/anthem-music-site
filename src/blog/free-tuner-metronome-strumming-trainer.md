A lesson is thirty minutes. The week is ten thousand. Most of what determines whether a student progresses doesn't happen in front of us, it happens on a Thursday afternoon in a living room, and that's the part we've never been able to reach.

So we built something that reaches it. It's free, it runs in a browser, and it's live right now at [anthemmusic.net/tuner](/tuner/).

Here's what it's actually for.

## "I forgot the pattern you showed me on Tuesday"

We hear this constantly, and it is not a discipline problem. A student learns a strumming pattern in a lesson, nails it while we're sitting there, and by Thursday it's gone. That's not laziness. That's how memory works when something is three days old and was only ever held in your hands, not written anywhere you can get back to.

The usual fix is a notebook, and notebooks get lost, or the student writes down "down down up up down" and has no idea what that felt like.

So the First Chords section is built as a path rather than a reference. Guitar, standard ukulele, and baritone ukulele each have their own track, with beginner paths for general and youth students, and it moves one objective at a time: chord shapes, then transitions, then strumming, then familiar progressions. A student can open it on Thursday and land exactly where they were on Tuesday, and hear it, not just read it.

## "I'll just wing the strumming"

This is the one that quietly costs people years.

A student learns a few chords, gets excited, and starts playing songs with a strumming hand that's doing roughly whatever it wants. The chords are right. The rhythm is approximate. And because the song is still recognizable, nothing forces the issue, so the habit sets.

We've said it before and we'll keep saying it: rhythm is the part that separates people who can play from people who know chords. It's the hardest thing to retrofit into a teenager or an adult who has been winging it for two years, and it's the easiest thing in the world to build correctly from the start.

That's why strumming isn't a separate toy in our app. It lives inside First Chords, because the pattern and the chord shape have to learn to work together, and neither one means much alone. The patterns build from practice exercises up through common patterns, two-bar patterns, and sixteenth-note patterns. Pick a chord inside an exercise and you'll hear that pattern played with the shape you're currently learning. Tap the chord name and the diagram comes up.

The metronome sits alongside it with tap tempo, subdivisions, and a choice of click, wood block, or drums, plus the tempo markings so students start learning what Andante and Allegro actually feel like rather than treating them as vocabulary words.

## "I have to buy a subscription to tune my ukulele"

This is the one that irritates us most.

Tuning is the first barrier a beginner hits and the one most likely to end the whole thing. An instrument that sounds wrong makes a student think they're playing wrong. Plenty of kids have quit over an out-of-tune guitar and told their parents they just didn't like it.

And the tools that should solve this are a mess. Half of them want a subscription. The ones that don't are buried in ads. And a surprising number simply don't know what to do with the instrument actually sitting in your house, so the moment you own something slightly outside the default, you're on your own.

Ours covers guitar, ukulele, bass, violin, banjo, saxophone, and a chromatic mode for everything else. Alternate tunings are in there too: Drop D, half step down, whole step down, Open G, Open D, and DADGAD. You can tap any string to hear the reference note, which is how most young students tune best, or turn on Auto Detect and use the live pitch trail, which shows you in cents whether you're flat or sharp and goes green when you're close.

No account to create. Nothing to subscribe to. Microphone audio stays on the device and is only used for live tuning.

## How to actually use it

Put it on the phone. On most phones you can add it to your home screen from the browser menu and it behaves like any other app.

Then the honest advice: tune before every single practice session, even when it sounds fine. It takes forty seconds and it's the difference between a student who enjoys practicing and one who doesn't know why their playing sounds bad.
